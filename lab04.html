Tableau Visualization

Reflection & Analysis

Data Preparation

I originally attempted to upload a few different datasets from Kaggle into Tableau, but many of them were too simple and did not have enough data for me to create a meaningful visualization. A lot of the datasets were missing key fields to create reliable charts. Once I switched to the “University Rankings” dataset from Kaggle, I was able to create readable and interesting graphs because of the sufficient data. This dataset did not need any adjustments or troubleshooting while uploading.

Design Choices

The first visualtization I created was a bar chart ranking average score by country. This was to compare how much each country’s universities score on average in the rankings. My second graph was a scatterplot that put research, in the form of publications, against quality of education. To better visualize this graph I color coded it by country and changed the size based on overall score. This graph was to visually examine the relationships between research performance and the quality of education across universities. My last graph was a line chart that shows the world rank of individual universities over time. For this graph you can select universities to see the comparison. This shows how individual universities ranking changed over time and observed trends over the years.

Expected Patterns

Before looking at the visualizations, I expected countries with historically strong higher education systems, such as the USA and UK, to have the top average scores. I also assumed there would be a positive relationship between publications and quality of education as universities with strong research would generally have higher scores. On the line chart, I expected the well-established universities to have more stable rankings, where others may have more variability in the charts.

Surprises/Outliers

One surprise was in the scatterplot. I noticed some universities that scored relatively high in research did not score as strongly in quality of education. They appeared out of the trend of the rest of the chart. Another unexpected pattern was in the line chart, I would have assumed most universities to stay constant. For example, the University of Colorado Boulder world rank moved from 46 to 41 to 63 to 54, which I find fairly unstable. Although these did not match my initial assumptions, there was nothing crazy out of the ordinary.

Questions for Further Investigation

What external factors might explain sudden changes in world rank over time?

Do certain countries focus more on research versus quality of education?

Are there any regional patterns that should be taken into effect by these outcomes?

Tool Evaluation

I believe Tableau Public is fairly easy to navigate once you understand the basics of creating the graphs. It can handle large datasets without errors and lagging, which is an advantage to the user. It takes a lot of trial and error to decide which rows and columns make the most sense together to create a meaningful graph, but I think it is a tool that can be extremely helpful.
